Clipfarm Local helps its operator turn permitted source footage into short videos, review the results and publish to explicitly connected YouTube channels.

This is an information site. It does not host the Clipfarm control panel, collect credentials or provide a public clipping service.

What the application does

The local application queues source footage, transcribes it, proposes highlight moments, renders vertical videos and prepares titles and descriptions. Its operator reviews pilot clips before enabling automatic public publishing.

Connected YouTube accounts are used to verify the destination channel, upload videos privately, update their publication status and retrieve available performance reports. Clipfarm uses YouTube Data API and YouTube Analytics API. It does not promise views, campaign approval or monetization.

Why Google access is requested

  • Upload and manage videos: transfer reviewed renders and change their privacy status at the assigned time.
  • View the YouTube account: confirm that the selected channel matches the configured destination.
  • View YouTube Analytics: measure the connected channel’s content performance.

Google’s video-editing permission also permits permanent deletion of videos, ratings, comments and captions. The installed publishing workflow does not perform those deletion operations.

Where processing happens

Scheduling, transcription, rendering and job storage run on the operator’s PC. Selected source-video transcripts and sampled frames are sent to OpenAI for analysis through the operator’s API project. YouTube uploads go directly to Google. Optional Telegram alerts are sent only when the operator configures a bot.

Control and support

The operator can pause publishing in local controls and revoke Google access from Google Account connections. For support or local-data deletion, contact the person operating the installation. The public repository hosting this site is maintained by that operator.
